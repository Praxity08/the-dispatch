# Shreya's Voice Guide — The Dispatch

This is the editorial voice for everything published under **The Dispatch**, Shreya's weekly newsletter on the Indian startup ecosystem. Every draft (whether Shreya writes it or the system drafts it for her review) should read as if she wrote it herself.

---

## 1. Who's writing, and who's reading

**Shreya** has covered Indian startups for years. She reads the term sheets, the regulatory filings and the fine print, and she's seen enough cycles to know what's noise. She's not a cheerleader and not a cynic. She's the friend in the industry who tells you what actually matters.

**The reader** is one of ~2,200 founders, operators, investors, journalists and policy people. They're busy and smart, and they've already seen the headline on X or LinkedIn. They open The Dispatch (38% open rate) for **the angle they didn't get anywhere else.**

> Rule of thumb: if the reader could have written the commentary after reading the headline, it isn't worth sending.

---

## 2. What makes a story Dispatch-worthy

Shreya picks stories that are at least one of:

1. **Counterintuitive.** It cuts against the popular narrative ("everyone's talking about the funding winter, but this sector just had its best quarter").
2. **Under-reported.** Few outlets covered it, or everyone covered the wrong part of it.
3. **A connection.** It links two trends nobody else has put side by side.
4. **A real signal.** It tells you where money, regulation or talent is actually moving, not where press releases say it is.

She skips routine funding announcements with no story behind them, PR-driven "launches", award ceremonies, listicles and anything that's purely a stock-price move.

---

## 3. How a commentary is built (2–3 lines)

Every story gets **2–3 sentences, roughly 40–70 words**:

| Line | Job | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The "so what"** | Why this matters, in one sentence. Not a summary of the headline. | Direct, confident |
| **2. The angle** | The non-obvious read: the second-order effect, the number that doesn't add up, the connection to another trend. | Sharp, specific |
| **3. (Optional) The kicker** | A pointed question, a prediction, or what to watch next. | Dry, sometimes wry |

Never restate the headline. The reader can see it right above the commentary.

---

## 4. Tone

- **Plain-spoken and confident.** Short sentences. Active voice. Say what you think.
- **Sceptical, not cynical.** Question the narrative; don't sneer at founders.
- **Specific over general.** Use numbers, names and dates. "₹332 Cr at a ₹1,050 Cr valuation" beats "a big round".
- **Dry wit is welcome, sparingly.** One wry line per issue is plenty. Never punch down.
- **Insider, not jargon-heavy.** Use terms the ecosystem uses (ARR, burn, down round, Series B, ESOP, MDR, DRHP), but never to sound clever.
- **Fair.** If you're critical of a company, the criticism must rest on facts in the story.
- **Indian context first.** Explain what this means for Indian founders, investors or users, even when the story is global.

---

## 5. Words and formatting

**Money and numbers**
- Rupees: `₹450 Cr`, `₹85 Cr`, `₹1.2 lakh Cr`. Use Cr and lakh, not "4.5 billion rupees".
- Dollars: `$75 Mn`, `$2 Bn`.
- Percentages as numerals: `7%`, `265% YoY`.
- Quarters and years: `Q3 FY26`, `FY31`.

**Do**
- Use first person sparingly and naturally ("I'd watch…", "My read:").
- Address the reader occasionally ("If you're raising a Series A this year…").
- Name the people and companies involved.
- End with a question only when it's a real question.

**Never**
- Emojis, exclamation marks, ALL CAPS or hashtags.
- Hype words: *game-changer, disruptive, revolutionary, unicorn-in-the-making, skyrocket, massive, huge*.
- AI-sounding filler: *delve, landscape, ecosystem is abuzz, in today's fast-paced world, it remains to be seen, only time will tell, navigate, unlock, leverage, robust, a testament to*.
- Clickbait ("You won't believe…") or vague teasers.
- Stating speculation as fact. Use "suggests", "my read is", or "if this holds".
- Copying sentences from the source article.

---

## 6. Newsletter structure

**Subject line:** `The Dispatch #<issue> — <the sharpest story of the week, in under 8 words>`
Example: `The Dispatch #104 — Why fintechs fear a UPI fee`

**Opening (2–3 sentences):** the week's theme in one breath, i.e. what connects this week's stories. No "Hello readers!" and no weather talk.

**Stories (8–10):** for each, the headline (linked), the source in brackets, then Shreya's 2–3 line commentary. Spread across at least three topic areas: a whole issue on funding rounds is a failure.

**Sign-off:** `— Shreya` followed by a one-line ask (reply with tips, forward to a friend). Then the footer with the unsubscribe link.

---

## 7. Examples

These use well-known past stories, for tone only.

**✅ Good: RBI restricts Paytm Payments Bank (Jan 2024)**
> Everyone's framing this as a Paytm story. It's really a message to every fintech that treated a banking licence as a growth hack: compliance is the product now, not the paperwork behind it. Watch how fast the "bank-in-a-box" partnerships get re-papered over the next two quarters.

**✅ Good: PhonePe moves its domicile from Singapore to India (2022)**
> Paying roughly $1 Bn in taxes to come home sounds irrational until you look at where the IPO money is. Indian public markets now reward Indian companies, and PhonePe just priced that in. Expect "reverse flipping" to become a board-meeting agenda item.

**✅ Good: Zepto raises $665 Mn (June 2024)**
> The round size is the headline; the real story is that late-stage investors have stopped worrying about burn. Quick commerce has convinced them it's a distribution business, not a delivery one, and they're paying for the shelf space. The question is whether kiranas or Amazon blink first.

**❌ Bad: same Zepto story**
> Zepto has raised a massive $665 million in a game-changing round! This is a testament to the robust quick commerce landscape in India. It remains to be seen how the company will leverage this funding.

*Why it fails:* it restates the headline, uses hype and filler, has an exclamation mark, and offers no angle.

---

## 8. Final checklist before a story goes in

- [ ] Does line 1 say why it matters, without repeating the headline?
- [ ] Is there one specific, non-obvious angle?
- [ ] 2–3 sentences, 40–70 words?
- [ ] Numbers in ₹ Cr / $ Mn format?
- [ ] No banned words, emojis or exclamation marks?
- [ ] Would a founder forward this line to their co-founder?
