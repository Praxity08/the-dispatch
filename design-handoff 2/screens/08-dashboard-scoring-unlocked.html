How a story gets its score

Every relevant story this week gets a score out of 100. It measures how well the story fits what The Dispatch is known for: news that is counterintuitive, under-reported, or connects two trends nobody else has linked. The score decides the shortlist; you make the final call.

  1. Gemini reads the week. Stories go to Gemini Flash in batches of 70, together with the headline of every other story this week, so it can spot connections between them.
  2. Five criteria are scored 0–5 by Gemini; one is counted by code. Gemini also writes the one-line "why it was picked" you see on each story. Under-reported is not a judgement call: the code counts how many different outlets ran the story.
  3. Scores become points. Each criterion has a weight. Its points are weight × score ÷ 5, so a 5/5 on Signal is worth 30 points and a 3/5 is worth 18. The six add up to a total out of 100.
  4. Adjustments. −10 points if every article in the story comes from a little-known outlet. Stories already covered in the last 4 issues are excluded entirely.
  5. Gemini is told to be a demanding editor: most stories should land at 1–3 on each criterion, and 5 is for the exceptional. That keeps the top of the list meaningful.

From scores to a lineup

  1. The top 25 stories by score form the shortlist.
  2. Gemini, acting as editor, picks 8–10 of them (aiming for 9) and puts them in running order. It weighs the scores but also variety and connections between stories, and avoids an issue that is all funding rounds.
  3. Code then checks the hard rules: 8–10 stories, at least 3 topics, at most 3 per topic, at most 1 per company, nothing from the last 4 issues. If a rule is broken, Gemini is told exactly which one and tries again (up to 2 times); after that, code picks by score while enforcing the rules.
  4. The next 10 best stories become the bench, your swap options.

How commentary is checked

Automatic checks (pass / fail)

  • 2–3 sentences, 40–70 words
  • The first sentence doesn't just reword the headline
  • No banned words from your voice guide (game-changer, delve, landscape, leverage…)
  • No emojis, exclamation marks or hashtags
  • Money written as ₹ Cr / $ Mn / $ Bn

These run live as you type in the editor.

AI copy-editor review (1–5 each)

  • Angle: says something non-obvious, more than a summary
  • Voice: sounds like your voice guide
  • Grounded: every fact is in the source articles
  • Forwardable: would a founder forward it to their co-founder?

The average must be at least 4. Anything that fails is rewritten once; if it still fails it's flagged ⚠ for you. Your own edits are never blocked.
